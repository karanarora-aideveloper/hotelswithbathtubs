Region-Aware Booking Plan

DRAFT for confirmation · 23 Oct 2026 · Nothing here is built yet. Items marked ASSUMPTION need the owner's yes or no.

The goal in one sentence. Each visitor sees the booking buttons that suit where they are, not one fixed list. A visitor in India sees MakeMyTrip, Booking.com and Agoda. A visitor in the US sees Expedia, Hotels.com, Trip.com and Booking.com. Visitors in the UK and Europe see platforms they use there.

1. What changes for a visitor

TodayAfter this plan
Everyone gets the same button order (Booking.com first, then Expedia, Hotels.com, Agoda, MakeMyTrip, others).The order and the set of buttons depend on the visitor's region.
The site only tells "India" from "US" (plus a few country names).Six visitor groups (table 2), decided from the country code the site already receives.
Only 190 hotels have an Expedia link. Hotels.com and Vrbo have none.Each new platform gets verified hotel links, or no button for that hotel.

3. Decisions I am assuming (please confirm each)

A. Visitor location decides the buttons, not the hotel's location. A US visitor viewing a hotel in India still sees Expedia first, if that hotel has an Expedia link. If it does not, the next platform in their list is used.
B. Show 2 or 3 buttons per hotel (the best matches for the visitor's region), not all of them.
C. A hotel with no verified link for a platform simply skips that button. We never fall back to a search page.
D. Booking.com is the global fallback and appears for every region where the hotel has a verified link.
E. We use the visitor's country as the site already gets it (the geo function), with no extra tracking and no personal data.
F. Data comes first, then display. Showing a platform before its links are verified would repeat the dead-link problem.

6. Risks

  • Expedia, Hotels.com and Trip.com are harder to read automatically than Booking.com. If a platform blocks automated checks, we use its official affiliate tool or feed, and we do not try to get around its protection.
  • Every new platform repeats the dead-link problem unless checked the same way.
  • More buttons means more affiliate-disclosure text is needed on pages.
